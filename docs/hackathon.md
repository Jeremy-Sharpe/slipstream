## Preliminary submission requirements

- Track name: include in the project description which track you are submitting to.
- Public codebase: uploaded to a public repository (e.g. GitHub) accessible to judges for review.
- Production URL: deployed and accessible via a live URL. Projects only running on localhost will be scored lower than a live, hosted deployment.
- 3 to 5 minute video: a walkthrough demonstrating the project's functional features in action. It should clearly show the app working end-to-end, not just slides or narration over static screens.

3 to 5 minute live demo plus speech: a short verbal pitch covering the problem, solution, and value proposition, paired with a live demonstration of the working product (not a pre-recorded video).

## Preliminary round: 80 points

Scored from: public codebase, production URL, 3 to 5 minute demo video.

### Technical Excellence: 30 points

Assessing the depth, quality, and the difficulty of your technical build.

**Functionality & Execution: 10 points.** The project works as intended, is stable and demonstrates a working end-to-end solution. The team should have a live app rather than a local host.

- 0-4: Mostly non-functional, static mockup, or breaks during the demo video.
- 5-6: Partially working. Some steps are faked, hardcoded, or need a specific demo path to succeed.
- 7-8: Works end-to-end with minor rough edges or known limitations the team is upfront about.
- 9-10: Fully working, polished, handles edge cases gracefully, no visible bugs in the demo video.

**Technical Difficulty: 8 points.** Depth of AI/ML engineering: fine-tuning, RAG pipelines, agentic/multi-step reasoning, custom evaluation, model chaining, embedding/vector search, etc.

- 0-2: Little to no meaningful technical work.
- 3-4: Mostly a wrapper on an existing tool/API with minimal custom logic.
- 5-6: Reasonable technical effort beyond a basic API call, some original engineering.
- 7-8: Sophisticated technical approach. The team clearly understands the "why" behind their architecture choices.

**Code Quality & Architecture: 6 points.** Repo is organised, readable, and reasonably documented (README, comments where needed). Sensible separation of concerns and appropriate tool/framework choices for the problem with the team able to justify them.

- 0-2: Disorganised, undocumented, or a single monolithic file with no structure.
- 3-4: Reasonably organised but with some clutter, duplication, or missing documentation.
- 5-6: Clean, modular, well-documented.

**Use of Data / Models: 6 points.** Appropriateness of model choice for the task (size, cost, latency vs. accuracy trade-offs), thoughtful handling of data quality, and any evaluation of output quality, even informal spot checks.

- 0-2: Model/data used without consideration of fit, quality, or limitations.
- 3-4: Reasonable choices but limited justification or evaluation.
- 5-6: Deliberate model/data choices with clear reasoning and some form of evaluation.

### Innovation: 25 points

Assessing originality and creative problem-solving.

**Originality of Idea: 10 points.** Is this a problem/approach judges haven't seen pitched before, or a common problem approached from a genuinely new angle?

- 0-4: Direct clone of an existing well-known product/tool with no meaningful change.
- 5-6: Familiar idea, minor twist.
- 7-8: Solid original spin on an existing concept.
- 9-10: Genuinely fresh idea or a well-known problem reframed in a surprising way.

**Creativity in Solution Design: 8 points.** Unexpected but effective use of AI capabilities, creative use of hackathon constraints, and design choices that make the AI feel genuinely useful rather than bolted on.

- 0-4: Generic, template-like solution design with no distinctive choices.
- 5-6: Some creative elements, but largely conventional design.
- 7-8: Creative, well-considered design decisions throughout the solution.

**Differentiation: 7 points.** Team can clearly name existing alternatives and articulate a specific, credible reason theirs is different or better.

- 0-2: No apparent awareness of existing solutions, or no real differentiation.
- 3-5: Some awareness of alternatives, differentiation is vague or partial.
- 6-7: Sharp, well-researched articulation of what sets them apart.

### Business Value & Application: 25 points

Assessing real-world relevance, feasibility, and impact.

**Problem Significance: 8 points.** The problem is clearly defined, with a specific target user/customer segment named and evidence the team understands their pain point.

- 0-4: Vague or unconvincing problem, unclear who actually has this problem.
- 5-6: Reasonable problem statement, audience is somewhat generic.
- 7-8: Sharp, well-evidenced problem statement with a clearly identified audience.

**Feasibility & Viability: 8 points.** Realistic view of what it would take to go from hackathon demo to real product: cost, data access, regulatory concerns, and adoption path where relevant.

- 0-4: No real consideration of how this would work outside the hackathon bubble.
- 5-6: Some feasibility thinking, but with gaps or unaddressed practical concerns.
- 7-8: Credible, well-thought-out path to real-world deployment; costs and constraints acknowledged.

**Impact & Value Proposition: 9 points.** Clear articulation of the specific value delivered (time saved, cost reduced, new capability unlocked, etc.), ideally with a rough estimate, and understanding of who benefits and how much.

- 0-4: Value proposition is generic, hand-wavy, or missing.
- 5-7: Clear value proposition but lacking specificity or evidence.
- 8-9: Compelling, specific, at least semi-quantified value proposition.

## Finals: 20 points

Scored from: live demo plus presentation (shortlisted teams only, Closing Night).

### Presentation & Communication: 20 points

Assessing how effectively the team communicates the problem, solution, and demo, live, in front of judges.

**Clarity of Pitch: 6 points.** Clean narrative arc: problem, solution, how it works, why it matters, within the time limit, with no unexplained jargon.

- 0-2: Confusing, unstructured, or the core idea never becomes clear.
- 3-4: Understandable but disorganised, rushed, or overly technical in places.
- 5-6: Crisp, well-structured, and accessible pitch that lands within time.

**Live Demo Quality: 8 points.** Demo is rehearsed, highlights the most impressive/differentiating feature, and actually proves the claims made in the pitch.

- 0-4: Demo fails, is skipped, or doesn't actually show the product working.
- 5-6: Functional demo with some fumbling or unclear moments.
- 7-8: Slick, confident, clearly shows the product working and proves the key claims.

**Team Engagement & Q&A: 6 points.** Answers are direct and specific, multiple team members can speak to the project, and the team is honest about limitations when pressed.

- 0-2: Struggles to answer basic questions about their own project.
- 3-4: Adequate answers but reliant on one team member, or some vagueness under pressure.
- 5-6: Confident, specific answers; clear shared understanding across the team.

## Tracks

State the track in the submission description.

### Track 1: Improve an Existing Business Capability

Take something a business already does and make it significantly better using AI. Choose an existing business function, workflow, or process and build a technical solution that improves how it operates: faster, cheaper, more accurate, more scalable, easier to use, or capable of producing better outcomes. The focus is on augmenting or transforming an existing capability, rather than inventing an entirely new one.

Examples: an AI customer-support agent that can resolve complex requests end-to-end; an intelligent document-processing pipeline for invoices and contracts; an AI coding or QA system for a software team; a sales assistant that researches and qualifies leads; a multimodal inventory-management system; an internal knowledge agent that can search company information and take actions; an AI system that improves demand forecasting or scheduling.

Think: "Businesses already do X. How can AI make X dramatically better?"

### Track 2: Create a New Business Capability

Build something that allows a business to do something it previously could not realistically do. Use AI to create a new product, service, workflow, interface, or organisational capability. The focus is on unlocking new possibilities, rather than simply optimising an existing process.

Examples: an AI agent that autonomously negotiates with suppliers; a voice-based system that lets customers dynamically create personalised products; an AI research team that continuously investigates new markets; a system that turns unstructured company data into interactive simulations; an autonomous service that operates on behalf of customers; a new AI-native product that would not have been practical without modern models.

Think: "What can a business do now that simply wasn't possible before?"

### Track 3: Solve a Business Problem

Start with a meaningful business problem and build the best technical solution you can. Identify a concrete problem faced by businesses, employees, customers, or industries and use AI as part of the solution. You do not need to begin with an existing capability or a particular AI technology. Start with the problem.

Examples: reducing food or inventory waste; detecting fraud or unusual transactions; helping small businesses understand cash flow; reducing employee scheduling conflicts; identifying supply-chain risks; making complex regulations easier to navigate; matching businesses with suitable suppliers; reducing customer churn; improving accessibility of business services.

Think: "What is a real problem worth solving, and what can we build in 48 hours to attack it?"

### Special Track: Built With ElevenLabs

Open to projects from any of the three main tracks; enter it alongside Track 1, 2 or 3. Use ElevenLabs as a meaningful part of your system. Your use of ElevenLabs should contribute to the core functionality of your project, rather than simply being added as an extra feature.

Examples: a real-time conversational voice agent for customers or employees; an AI sales or support agent capable of natural spoken interaction; a multilingual voice interface; a voice-driven business workflow where users complete tasks entirely through conversation; an interactive training or onboarding system with dynamic AI voices; a system that generates personalised audio content at scale.

Think: "What becomes possible when AI can naturally speak, listen, and interact through voice?"
